# Questions for the client

Kwame is unavailable before the review, so none of these will be answered.
Each row records where the question came from, why it matters to the
recommendation, and the default I applied. Each default is an assumption;
the major ones have a decision note.

| ID | Source | Question | Why it matters | My default | Recorded in |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | Brief | What will Adjoa's laptop have: which operating system, which Python version, and internet access during meetings? | She must rerun the analysis without me, sometimes live in a meeting. If it needs the internet and she has none, it fails in front of the board. | Tested on Windows with Python 3.14 and 3.15. Analysis runs from the cached responses, so no internet is needed in a meeting; fetching is a separate first run. | ADR-002, README |
| Q2 | Brief | Which films count as franchise films? Is being in a TMDb collection enough, and what about a collection with only one of its films on our list? | It decides which group every film is in, so it changes both the franchise and the standalone figures, and the shortlist. | Any film TMDb puts in a collection is a franchise film, even if only one of its films is on the list. The result is also shown with the 9 single-film collections counted as standalone. | ADR-005 |
| Q3 | Brief | How do we measure a film's success? | It decides which group looks stronger. A measure that ignores cost favours big-budget films, which tend to be franchise films. | Return multiple: revenue divided by budget. The headline is the median per group; the mean and median revenue are shown beside it. | ADR-003 |
| Q4 | Brief | Is a budget or revenue of 0 a real zero or a missing value? | A false zero pulls a group's average down, and a 0 budget makes return impossible to calculate. The direction of the recommendation could change. | Treated as missing: excluded from return figures only, counted and reported. None in the 2026-11-20 snapshot. | ADR-003; data diagnosis (false alarms) |
| Q5 | Brief | Which date puts a film in the 2000-onward window, and what happens to films with no date? | It decides which films are in each window. A wrong date near 2000 moves a film between windows. | TMDb's release_date field. A film with no date stays in all years and is left out of 2000 onward, with the count reported. 7 films fall in 1999 or 2000. | ADR-004; data diagnosis (F10) |
| Q6 | Brief | If the two windows give different answers, which one leads the memo? | Kwame needs one answer he can defend, and Yaw will challenge anything before 2000. | 2000 onward leads, decided before any results were computed. All years is shown beside it, and a flip would be stated plainly. In the 2026-11-20 snapshot the answer does not flip. | ADR-004 |
| Q7 | Brief | What makes a franchise or a standalone film one of the strongest for the shortlist? How is a whole franchise scored? | Bundles are bought whole, so the way a franchise is scored decides which three make the shortlist. | A franchise is scored by the median return of its films from 2000 onward, and needs at least 2 such films to be ranked. Its weakest film and all-years figures are shown beside it. Standalones are ranked by return within 2000 onward; older ones are listed separately. | ADR-004, ADR-005 |
| Q8 | Brief | Who counts as a lead actor and a director, and how many films must a person have before they count as a reliable signal? | With too few films, one hit makes a name look reliable, and the negotiators would be using a false lever. | Lead actors are the top three billed. Directors come from the crew list, and co-directors each count fully. A name needs at least 3 films across at least 2 different series. | ADR-005 |
| Q9 | Brief | Should older figures be adjusted for inflation? | Unadjusted, older films look weaker in money terms, which can tilt the all-years comparison. Yaw will raise it. | No adjustment. Budget and revenue are in the same era's money, so the return multiple largely cancels inflation. The re-release effect on old films is handled by leading with 2000 onward. | ADR-003, ADR-004 |
| Q10 | Brief | The deal note must say what to pay for the strongest franchise relative to the other candidates. Relative to which films, and by which measure? | It is the one number Kwame takes into the negotiation. | The franchise's 2000-onward median return as a multiple of the 2000-onward standalone median, with its rank among the other franchises. | ADR-004, ADR-005 |
| Q11 | Brief | How do we treat an id listed more than once, two ids that look like the same film, and an id that no longer exists? | Repeated films count twice in every average. Deleting a film that only looks like a duplicate removes real data. | The same id listed twice is kept once and the copy logged. IMDb ids are converted with TMDb's find lookup, then checked for repeats. Values that are not ids are excluded. Ids TMDb doesn't know (404) are recorded, not retried. Two ids that look like the same film are checked on every run; none found. Every row is listed in data/output/id_account.csv. | Data diagnosis (F1 to F4) |

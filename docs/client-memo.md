# Memo: franchise bundles or standalone films

**To:** Kwame Boateng, VP Content Acquisition
**From:** Irene Abbey
**Data as of:** 20 November 2026 (film figures as published on TMDb that day)

## Recommendation

**Lean towards franchise bundles, but choose them one at a time and do not pay for a franchise name alone.** On the films we were asked to assess, franchise films have earned more for every dollar spent than standalone films. That holds however the figures are cut, but the size of the advantage is uncertain, so treat it as a tilt, not a rule.

## The evidence

For films released from 2000 onward, a typical franchise film earned **5.1 times its production cost** at the box office (63 films); a typical standalone film earned **3.8 times** (8 films). Franchise films come out about **1.3 times** stronger.

Across all years the gap is similar: 5.6 times against 3.8 times. **The answer does not change between the two periods.** We lead with 2000 onward because older figures include decades of re-releases and older accounting, and the strongest results on the list (Star Wars, Jaws, The Godfather) all come from before 1995.

The advantage holds in every version we tested, but it ranges from **1.1 to 1.7 times** depending on how borderline films are counted. The standalone side rests on only 8 recent films, so one or two titles can move it.

## Shortlist to negotiate from

**Franchises** (typical return from 2000 onward, and the weakest film in the bundle):

1. **The Lord of the Rings** (3 films): 11.7 times; weakest film 9.4 times.
2. **Ice Age** (3 films): 8.3 times; weakest 6.5 times.
3. **The Avengers** (4 films): 6.9 times; weakest 6.0 times, but the most expensive bundle on the list.

**Standalone films:** Spirited Away (14.5 times), Inception (5.2), Interstellar (4.5), Memento (4.5), The Departed (3.2).

Star Wars ranks first if older films are counted, but only fourth on its films from 2000 onward. Pulp Fiction, Forrest Gump and Schindler's List are strong only on figures from before 2000.

## Talent as a lever

On recent films, **Peter Jackson** (director, 6 films, typical return 6.7 times) and **Robert Downey Jr.** (lead, 7 films, 6.1 times) have the most consistent records, though each is tied to closely related series (Middle-earth; Marvel). **Christopher Nolan** directed 7 films across 5 series with a solid but lower 4.5 times. Treat older names with care: Steven Spielberg's and Harrison Ford's records rest almost entirely on films from before 2000.

## Deal note: The Lord of the Rings

This is the strongest franchise on the list. All three films are from 2000 onward, and even its weakest film returned more than 9 times its cost. **The number that drove this view: its typical film returned 11.7 times its cost, three times the typical standalone film.** It justifies paying more per title than for a standalone film. Because that figure rests on three films and on box office rather than streaming, I would not pay more than about twice the per-title price of a comparable standalone.

## What I could not stand behind

- **Box office is not streaming value.** It is the best available signal of demand, but not what StreamVault will earn. Your own viewing data would be a better measure.
- **Every film on the list was a hit.** The list contains no flops, so it cannot fully test the concern about weak middle films in a bundle.
- **8 of the 127 candidates could not be assessed** (5 no longer exist in the database, 3 had no usable id), and 7 were duplicates. 112 films were assessed.
- **What would change the recommendation:** if more recent standalone films were added and their typical return rose above about 5 times, the advantage would disappear.
